# Secondary and Analytical Sources

## Politics1

[Politics1 South Carolina](https://politics1.com/sc.htm) is useful for discovering candidates, campaign sites, and possible field changes. It is updated frequently but can contain stale or incomplete status information. Use it for leads, not final ballot status.

## Race ratings

[Cook Political Report](https://www.cookpolitical.com/), [Sabato's Crystal Ball](https://centerforpolitics.org/crystalball/), and [Inside Elections](https://insideelections.com/) provide dated editorial race ratings and analysis. Use ratings only on race pages, with the source and date. Do not put them in candidate biographies or treat them as facts about a candidate.

## Track AIPAC

[Track AIPAC](https://www.trackaipac.com/) is an advocacy organization with explicit political goals and fundraising links. Its claims may identify FEC records worth checking, but its figures should not be treated as neutral evidence or independent confirmation of the Influence Registry.

## FollowTheMoney

[FollowTheMoney.org](https://www.followthemoney.org/) states that it merged into OpenSecrets, is no longer actively maintained, and its state data is current only through the 2024 election year. Keep it for historical leads only. Do not build new 2026 workflows around it.

## Voteview

[Voteview](https://voteview.com/) can provide derived analysis of congressional voting patterns and ideological positioning. Cite the underlying official roll-call vote first and label Voteview's interpretation as analysis.

## OpenStates / Plural

[OpenStates](https://www.openstates.org/) is now part of Plural's open legislative-data project. It may be useful for automation, but the official South Carolina Legislature remains the preferred source for this wiki's state legislative claims.

## Integration rule

These sources belong in research notes or specific race pages only when they answer a question the primary sources cannot. None should replace SC Votes, Anderson County, the FEC, the South Carolina Ethics Commission, Congress.gov, or the South Carolina Legislature.
